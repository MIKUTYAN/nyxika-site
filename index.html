About me

I spent a long time searching for what truly felt like me

When I was a child, my parents enrolled me in art school because I drew everywhere — on the walls, furniture, and in my notebooks. I created my own manga, made little notebooks for it, and could spend hours drawing characters.

Then I grew up, and the inevitable question came: "What am I going to do with my life?"

I tried programming, photography, and even enrolled in an art university. But then the war began, I had to move, and everything changed again.

After that came nail art, epoxy resin, and eventually almost five years of web design. I worked with people and teams, creating real projects — but somewhere inside, I still felt that it wasn't quite me.

And one day, I simply started drawing characters again. Not for work. Not because I had to. Just because I wanted to.

That's when I realized: no matter how many different paths I tried, I always found my way back to drawing.

Now I finally allow myself to do what I truly love — creating characters, giving them stories, and putting a little piece of myself into each one.

Sometimes you have to take a very long journey to finally find your way back to yourself.

Why it keeps getting better

I design these for you, not at you

Nothing I draw is random. Before a new character exists, I go back through what you saved, what you bid on, and which messages said "oh my god, she's perfect." That's my brief.

So every drop lands a little closer than the last one — the palettes you reach for, the archetypes you keep coming back to, the small details you notice and take the time to tell me about.

I know that isn't how most artists work. But you are the reason this is my job now, and I would rather make the thing you actually wanted than the thing I assumed you did.

I love you all, and I just want to keep making more for you.
